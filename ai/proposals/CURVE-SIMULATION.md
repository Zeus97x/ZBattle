# Campaign difficulty simulation (D-CURVE)

> **Proposal for Zeus97x's approval. Opponent stats are not live.** The runtime still ships only `area-00/slot-0`. Nothing here changes rewards, XP or the approved layout.

Updated 2026-10-10 America/Toronto. Owner: Claude. Decision basis: D-CURVE (batch 2): "Simulate the actual three-member party, switching, evolution and equipment before finalising opponent stats; bosses should provide a meaningful challenge."

## How it was simulated
- **Engine:** the real `BattleEngine` (rules revision 3), with items, switching and replacement exactly as in the game. Code: `core/src/test/.../battle/CurveSimulation.kt`. The report is regenerated by `CurveSimulationTest.writeReport`, which writes `core/build/curve-simulation.md`.
- **Layout and XP:** `ai/proposals/campaign-proposal.json` revision 2, with 300 encounters and the approved XP (16 240 first-win XP).
- **Player:** "on curve". Before each encounter, every member holds a third of all earlier first-win XP (D-PARTY-XP split), so the level is 1 + xp/100, capped at 50.
- **Parties:** all **84 trios** of distinct families that have artwork, all **Baby** form.
- **Play styles:**
  - **Auto:** the B1 policy, Skill when ready, no items.
  - **Prepared:** auto plus a Potion below 35% HP (at most 5 per battle), a switch below 25% HP to a member above 60%, and a **Fang Charm I** on every member.
  - **Prepared II:** the same, with **Fang Charm II**.
- **Reading the numbers:** each cell is the win % over the 84 trios, with the average party HP left on a win in brackets. Lower HP left means a harder fight.

## Limits (flagged)
- **Evolution is not simulated.** D-EVOLUTION-THRESHOLDS is open (batch 2 #11). Evolved forms have much higher base stats (Young 6/5/7, finals up to 12/10/6 or 7/7/14, against Baby 4/4/4), so every number below is the **hardest** case. Once thresholds exist, this has to be re-run.
- **Same base stats:** all Baby forms share base stats, so trios differ only by family advantage and Burn/Weaken. The engine is deterministic, so a group tends to win or lose together. HP left is therefore the more useful measure of challenge.
- **On-curve only.** A player who skips fights or grinds replays sits below or above these levels.

## Result 1: the B4 proposal stats are far too easy
Every kind is won 100% of the time in every group, and bosses leave about as much HP as wilds:

Overall: Wild 100% (78% HP) · MiniBoss 100% (74% HP) · StageBoss 100% (72% HP) · LocationBoss 100% (69% HP) · RegionBoss 100% (65% HP) (auto)

Overall: Wild 100% (80% HP) · MiniBoss 100% (77% HP) · StageBoss 100% (77% HP) · LocationBoss 100% (77% HP) · RegionBoss 100% (76% HP) (prepared)

The wild ramp itself is well shaped: HP left stays at about 78% from group 0 to group 11, so player growth keeps pace with the area ramp. The problem is the **flat boss bonuses** (+1–3 power, +8–25 HP). They are large next to group-0 stats and negligible by group 11, and a party of three has three times the HP pool of one opponent.

## Result 2: multiplicative boss scaling keeps the challenge even
The candidates keep the wild ramp unchanged and multiply a boss's HP and power/guard by a fixed percentage per kind, so difficulty stays even from group 0 to group 11. A grid of flat-bonus scales was also searched; it produced 0%-to-100% swings between groups, which multiplicative scaling avoids.
